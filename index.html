Diagrammata Venniana Symmetrica

“No one in the long history of mathematics ever became better known for less.”

— William Dunham, The Mathematical Universe (1994), p. 262 (referring to Venn)

An n-curve Venn diagram has one connected region for each of the 2n ways a point can be inside or outside each curve. It's "simple" if no three curves meet at a point, and in that case it has 2n − 2 crossings. It's symmetric if rotating it by 1/n of a turn maps it onto itself.

A Venn diagram can only be symmetric if n is prime (Henderson 1963; complete proof by Wagon and Webb 2008; also n=1 is a trivial exception), and symmetric diagrams exist for every prime (Griggs, Killian, Savage 2004), but those aren't necessarily simple. Simple symmetric diagrams were known only up to n = 13 (Mamakani and Ruskey 2014) until Chris Dzoba, using Claude and Codex, found ones with 17 and 19 curves in September 2026 and with 23 in October.
